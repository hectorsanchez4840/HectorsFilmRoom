The Brief

Describe the starting point of this project. What was the goal? What problem were you trying to solve, or what story were you trying to tell? If it was a class project, what was the assignment? If it was client work, what did they come to you with?

Be specific. Hiring managers want to understand the constraints you were working within — budget, timeline, access to subjects, equipment limitations. Context makes the work more impressive, not less.

"Add a quote here — from your subject, your client, a professor, or even your own director's note about why this project mattered to you."

Any additional context about the brief goes here. What made this project challenging or interesting from the start?

02

The Approach

This is where you show how you think. What was your creative strategy? Why did you make the choices you made — visually, narratively, tonally? What were the alternatives you considered and why did you land where you did?

This is the section hiring managers read most carefully. Anyone can say they made a documentary. Not everyone can articulate WHY they approached it the way they did. Show your thinking here.

Talk about the visual language you chose, how you structured the story arc, why you interviewed who you interviewed, what you wanted the audience to feel and why.

03

The Process

Walk through how you actually made it. This doesn't need to be exhaustive — pick the moments that show your craft, your problem-solving, or your professionalism.

01

Pre-Production

How did you prepare? Research, treatment writing, location scouting, subject outreach, shot lists, interview prep. What did you do before you picked up the camera?

02

Production

What happened on the day(s) of filming? Who was involved, what equipment did you use, what challenges came up and how did you handle them?

03

Post-Production

How did you edit? What was your assembly process, how did you structure the story in the edit, what color grade or audio work did you do?

04

Delivery

How and where was this delivered? Screened in class, published online, submitted to a client, posted on social — what happened when it was done?

04

The Result

0K

Views

Add your view count if published online. If screened in class or privately, remove this stat.

A

Grade / Feedback

If this was academic work, what grade or feedback did you receive? For client work, what was their response?

Add

Your Own Metric

Any other outcome worth highlighting — shared by the subject, picked up somewhere, used by a client, etc.

Describe the outcome in your own words. What was the response — from the subject, your professor, a client, or an audience? What did this project lead to? What are you most proud of about how it turned out?

Even if there are no hard metrics, talk about the human result — did your subject feel seen? Did the piece spark a conversation? Did it get shared somewhere meaningful?

05

What I Learned

This section is optional but powerful — it shows self-awareness and a growth mindset, which hiring managers love. What would you do differently? What did this project teach you about your craft, your process, or yourself as a storyteller?
